# 07 Farm Infrastructure

## Core Infrastructure

The farm requires integrated physical infrastructure to support bird health, production efficiency, labor movement, storage, sanitation, and logistics. Infrastructure quality directly affects mortality, egg damage, hygiene standards, and maintenance cost.

## Major Components

- Layer sheds with proper ventilation and service access.
- Feed godown with moisture protection and rodent control.
- Egg room for collection, sorting, tray stacking, and dispatch staging.
- Medicine and consumables store.
- Water tanks, pipelines, and pressure-regulated distribution.
- Electrical room, backup system, and lighting circuits.
- Fencing, gate control, and visitor entry protocol point.
- Drainage network and manure handling area.

## Design Priorities

Buildings should emphasize durability, washability, airflow, and operational convenience rather than unnecessary cosmetic expense. Utility lines should be planned for maintenance access and future load expansion.

## Practical Advice

Investing in strong drainage, clean storage, and durable service infrastructure usually yields better operational outcomes than overspending on non-essential civil features. Infrastructure should be standardized, maintainable, and aligned with daily farm routines.
