## 2. Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)

---

### SOP-01: Raw Material Receiving & Logging

**When:** Every delivery of raw materials (cow dung, straw, neem cake, etc.)
**Who:** Operator 2 (with owner present for first few deliveries)
**Time required:** 20–30 minutes per delivery

**Steps:**

1. **Direct the vehicle** to the receiving pad (Zone A). Keep access road clear.
2. **Weigh each material type** on the platform scale before offloading if possible, or weigh after using tare method.
   - If bulk truck: estimate weight from truck capacity and fill level.
   - For cow dung in drums/trolleys: weigh per load and multiply.
3. **Record in the Raw Material Logbook** (see Section 10):
   - Date and time
   - Supplier name and contact
   - Material type
   - Quantity (kg)
   - Moisture condition (dry / moist / wet)
   - Any visible contamination (plastic, stones, disease) — reject if found
4. **Sort materials by type:**
   - Dry browns (straw, leaves, shells) → stack in shredding zone
   - Wet greens (cow dung, poultry litter) → store in shaded corner, use within 3 days
   - Neem cake / press mud → keep in covered store, use within 7 days
5. **Payment:** Record supplier, quantity, and rate for accounts.

**Rejection criteria:** Reject any material with visible plastic waste, dead animals, chemical contamination (oil/solvent smell), or heavily diseased crop material.

### SOP-02: Shredding & Pre-Soaking

**When:** Day before windrow construction (shred and soak the previous evening)
**Who:** Operator 2
**Time required:** 3.5–4 hours for one full windrow batch of dry material (~1,750 kg dry matter)

**Steps:**

1. **Check shredder** before starting:
   - Inspect blade condition (sharpen monthly)
   - Check belt tension
   - Ensure no blockage in discharge chute
   - Wear eye protection and gloves — mandatory
2. **Feed dry material** into shredder in small bundles (do not overfeed — listen for motor strain):
   - Paddy straw: break bales into loose handfuls, feed at steady pace
   - Stalks/dry leaves: feed 2–3 kg at a time
   - Target output size: **5–8 cm length**
3. **Collect shredded output** in a pile adjacent to shredder.
4. **Pre-soak in the soak pit:**
   - Fill soak pit with 400–500 L water + 50 kg fresh cow dung (make a slurry)
   - Submerge or pour slurry over shredded straw pile
   - Let soak overnight (minimum 8 hours, maximum 24 hours)
   - This softens the fibre and inoculates with microbes from the dung
5. **Morning before windrow build:** Check soaked straw — should be moist throughout (squeeze test: moist but not dripping). Add water if dry patches remain.

**Shredder daily output target:** 700 kg dry matter per day (3.5 hrs @ ~200 kg/hr)

---

### SOP-03: Windrow Construction

**When:** Every 3rd day (to maintain batch rotation)
**Who:** Operator 1 + Operator 2
**Time required:** 2.5–3 hours for one 4,200 kg windrow
**Materials needed (per windrow, Recipe 1):**

| Material | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Pre-soaked shredded straw (paddy/tur dal) | 1,764 kg (42%) |
| Fresh cow dung | 1,260 kg (30%) |
| Dry leaf litter | 588 kg (14%) |
| Neem cake (de-oiled) | 336 kg (8%) |
| Poultry litter | 168 kg (4%) |
| Press mud | 84 kg (2%) |

**Steps:**

1. **Mark the windrow footprint** on the ground:
   - Length: 5.0 m
   - Base width: 2.5 m
   - Use lime powder or rope to mark corners
   - Ensure 1 m clear access strip on all sides
2. **Lay the base layer (Layer 1):**
   - Spread coarse dry stalks/cane trash, 10 cm thick
   - This creates aeration channels at the base
3. **Build alternating layers from bottom:**

   | Layer | Material | Thickness |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1 | Coarse stalks (aeration base) | 10 cm |
   | 2 | Pre-soaked straw | 20 cm |
   | 3 | Cow dung + poultry litter (spread evenly) | 10 cm |
   | 4 | Leaf litter + neem cake (sprinkle) | 15 cm |
   | 5 | Pre-soaked straw | 15 cm |
   | 6 | Cow dung + press mud | 10 cm |
   | 7 | Thin soil or old compost (top seal) | 3 cm |

4. **Shape the windrow** into a trapezoid profile (2.5 m base, 0.8 m top, 1.5 m height) using a fork and spade — slope the sides so rain runs off rather than in.
5. **Check moisture** with squeeze test at 3 spots. Sprinkle water on any dry patches before sealing.
6. **Insert a bamboo stake** (marked with windrow number and date) at the centre top.
7. **Record in Windrow Logbook:**
   - Windrow number (sequential, e.g., W-001)
   - Date built
   - Batch weight (kg per material)
   - Initial moisture assessment
   - Location on site (row, position)
8. **Take first temperature reading** 48 hours after construction — temperature should be rising. Log it.

### SOP-04: Daily Monitoring

**When:** Every working day (Monday–Saturday), morning shift, 8:00–9:00 AM
**Who:** Operator 1
**Time required:** 45–60 minutes for all active windrows

**Steps:**

1. **Walk all active windrows** in Zone C (both rows). Do this in the same order every day.
2. **For each windrow, record in the Daily Log:**
   - Windrow number
   - Temperature reading: insert probe 40 cm deep at 3 points (left shoulder, centre top, right shoulder). Record all 3. Use the average.
   - Visual condition: colour of outer surface, any white fungal growth (normal — good sign), visible undecomposed material, cracking/drying edges
   - Smell: note if earthy (good), ammonia (add straw, turn soon), or rotten egg (turn immediately)
   - Moisture: do squeeze test on outer edge. Note if watering needed.
3. **Flag windrows needing action:**
   - Red flag stake = Turn today
   - Yellow flag stake = Add water today
   - Green flag stake = Check tomorrow
4. **Report to owner/manager** any windrow with temperature <45°C in active phase (Weeks 2–5) or smell problem.
5. **Check curing zone piles** — temperature and smell only, no turning unless instructed.

### SOP-05: Windrow Turning

**When:** Per schedule below, or when flagged in daily monitoring
**Who:** Operator 1 + Operator 2 (both required — physically demanding)
**Time required:** 1.5–2 hours per 5 m windrow

**Turning schedule:**

| Windrow Age | Turn No. | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Week 2 | Turn 1 | Day 10–12 |
| Week 3 | Turn 2 | Day 17–19 |
| Week 4 | Turn 3 | Day 24–26 |
| Week 5–6 | Turn 4 | Day 33–36 |
| Week 7–8 | Turn 5 | Day 45–50 |

**Steps:**

1. **Prepare the adjacent space** (1 m side strip) — clear of tools and materials.
2. **Start from one end** of the windrow. Use a 4-tine fork or front loader.
3. **Move material from the outer crust/edges to the new pile centre first** — this is the key step. Outer material is drier and less decomposed; it needs to go into the hot zone.
4. **Build the new windrow** alongside the original, shifting 0.5 m sideways.
5. **Check moisture as you turn:**
   - If outer edges are dry (dusty, light brown): spray water as you layer the new pile
   - If centre is wet and slimy: add dry straw as you rebuild
6. **Check for hot spots** (white/grey ash-like areas in centre = too hot, too dry).
7. **After turning, take temperature reading** at 3 spots. A properly turned windrow will reheat to 55°C+ within 24–48 hours.
8. **Record in Windrow Logbook:** Turn number, date, temperature before and after (24 hrs later), any material added, observations.

**Signs of a good turn:** New pile has a mixed texture, releases steam (normal), temperature drops briefly then rises again within 1–2 days.

### SOP-06: Moisture Management

**When:** As flagged in daily monitoring
**Who:** Operator 1 or 2
**Time required:** 20–30 minutes per windrow

**The squeeze test — your primary moisture tool:**

```
Grab a handful of compost from 30 cm inside the pile edge.
Squeeze firmly in a closed fist for 10 seconds.

Result          │ Interpretation       │ Action
────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────────────────
Water drips     │ Too wet (>65%)       │ Add dry straw, turn soon
freely          │                      │
────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────────────────
1–2 drops       │ IDEAL (55–60%)       │ No action needed
appear          │                      │
────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────────────────
Moist but no    │ Slightly dry (45–50%)│ Water during next turn
drops           │                      │ or probe and spot-water
────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────────────────
Dry, no         │ Too dry (<40%)       │ Water immediately — insert
cohesion        │                      │ hose into pile at 4–5 spots
                │                      │ or turn + water
```

**Watering method (without turning):**
1. Use a metal rod or thick bamboo to poke 5–6 holes (5 cm diameter, 60 cm deep) from the top of the windrow, spaced 1 m apart.
2. Insert hose into each hole and add 20–30 L per hole slowly.
3. Cap holes with loose compost.

**Summer protocol (March–May, Hyderabad temperature 38–43°C):**
- Check moisture daily — windrows can lose 5–8% moisture per day in peak summer
- Water every 2–3 days at minimum
- Prioritise watering in early morning (less evaporation)
- Keep shade net deployed over active windrows at all times

### SOP-07: Transfer to Curing Zone

**When:** When active windrow reaches end of Week 8 and passes maturity check
**Who:** Operator 1 + Operator 2
**Time required:** 1–1.5 hours

**Pre-transfer checks:**
- [ ] Temperature ≤ 40°C and falling
- [ ] Smell: earthy, no ammonia, no rotten egg
- [ ] Volume reduced to ~35% of original
- [ ] Material visibly darker and more uniform

**Steps:**
1. Do a full turn of the windrow to aerate before transfer.
2. Move the material to the designated curing slot in Zone D.
3. Build a lower, wider pile: maximum **0.8 m height**, spread wider for better aeration.
4. Tag with Windrow Number + Transfer Date.
5. Cover loosely with shade net (not tightly sealed).
6. Note in Windrow Logbook: transfer date, estimated weight, temperature at transfer.
7. **Do not water heavily** in curing zone — aim for 35–40% moisture (slightly drier than active phase).

### SOP-08: Maturity Testing

**When:** Week 10–11 (2–3 weeks after transfer to curing zone)
**Who:** Operator 1, results reviewed by owner
**Time required:** 15 minutes on-site + 5 days for germination test

**Run all 5 tests. Compost is ready only when ALL 5 pass.**

**Test 1 — Temperature Test**
- Insert probe to 30 cm depth in 3 spots
- Pass: temperature ≤ ambient air temperature + 5°C
- Fail: still >40°C, or reheats after turning → cure 1 more week

**Test 2 — Smell Test**
- Take handful, smell at close range
- Pass: earthy, soil-like, forest floor smell
- Fail: any ammonia, rotten egg, or sour smell → cure 1 more week

**Test 3 — Visual / Texture Test**
- Spread handful on white paper
- Pass: uniformly dark brown-black, crumbly, no visible straw or dung chunks
- Fail: identifiable original materials still present → cure 1 more week

**Test 4 — Bag (Seal) Test**
- Place 250 g compost in sealed plastic bag
- Leave in sun for 2 days
- Open and smell
- Pass: earthy smell, no foul odour
- Fail: foul, sour, or ammonia smell → cure 1 more week

**Test 5 — Germination Test (most important for quality)**
1. Fill 2 small clay pots:
   - Pot A: 100% finished compost (from this batch)
   - Pot B: 100% regular garden soil (control)
2. Plant 10 radish seeds in each, same depth (1 cm), same water
3. Keep both in same location, same sunlight, same watering
4. Count germinated seeds at Day 5 and Day 7
5. Pass: Compost pot shows ≥ 80% of control pot germination rate
6. Fail: <80% germination → phytotoxic compounds still present → cure 2 more weeks

**Record all 5 test results in Maturity Test Logbook.**

### SOP-09: Screening

**When:** After batch passes all 5 maturity tests
**Who:** Operator 2
**Time required:** 2.5–3 hours per ~3,500 kg cured compost (yields ~3,200 kg screened product)

**Steps:**
1. Set up the oscillating sieve screen (6–8 mm mesh) on a flat surface.
2. Move cured compost from Zone D to screening area (Zone E) in wheelbarrow loads.
3. Feed compost onto the sieve in small shovelfuls — do not overload.
4. **Fine fraction (passes through)** → collect in pile below screen or directly into storage bay.
5. **Coarse rejects (retained on screen)** → collect separately. These go back into the next fresh windrow as inoculant material (adds microbes, do not discard).
6. Weigh the screened compost output. Record in Batch Logbook.
7. Typical rejection rate: 8–12% by weight. If >15%, shredding was insufficient — adjust shredder settings.
8. Move screened compost to covered storage (Zone F) immediately. Do not leave exposed to direct rain or strong sun.

---

### SOP-10: Bagging & Dispatch

**When:** Against customer orders or to build stock
**Who:** Operator 2
**Time required:** 1–1.5 hours per ton

**Steps:**

1. **Weigh bags** on the platform scale:
   - 50 kg PP bags: fill to 51–52 kg (slight overfill for settling)
   - 25 kg bags: fill to 26 kg
   - 10 kg branded bags: fill to 10.5 kg
2. **Check moisture** before bagging — compost must be at 30–35% moisture. If too dry (dusty), lightly mist before bagging. If too wet (sticks in clumps), spread in sun for 30 minutes first.
3. **Seal bags** with bag-stitching machine or hand-tie with wire. Ensure no gaps.
4. **Label every bag:**
   - Product name: "Organic Compost"
   - Net weight
   - Batch number (format: YYYYMM-XX, e.g., 202604-03)
   - Manufactured by: [your farm name + address]
   - Date of manufacture
   - Best before: 6 months from manufacture
   - NPK content (from lab test if available)
5. **Stack bags** on pallets (if available) or on a raised wooden platform — never directly on wet ground.
6. **Issue a delivery challan** for every dispatch: buyer name, date, bag count, weight, rate, amount.
7. **Record in Dispatch Logbook:** Batch number, bags dispatched, total kg, buyer, price, payment status.

## 7. Seasonal Operating Adjustments

### Summer (March–May) — Hyderabad 38–43°C

| Parameter | Standard | Summer Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Watering frequency | Every 3–4 days | Every 1–2 days |
| Shade net | Optional | Mandatory over all active windrows |
| Windrow height | 1.5 m | Reduce to 1.2 m (less internal heat buildup) |
| Turning frequency | Every 7 days | Every 5 days |
| Work hours | 8 AM–5 PM | 6–11 AM, then 4–7 PM (avoid peak heat) |
| Moisture check | Once daily | Twice daily (morning + 3 PM) |
| Risk | Fire if pile overdries | Keep fire-fighting water point accessible |

### Monsoon (June–September) — Heavy rainfall, humidity 80–95%

| Parameter | Standard | Monsoon Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Windrow cover | Shade net | Full tarpaulin cover (keep sloped so rain runs off) |
| Drainage channels | Monthly clear | Clear after every heavy rain |
| Turning frequency | Every 7 days | Every 10 days (less drying needed) |
| Watering | As needed | Likely zero — monitor for excess moisture |
| New windrow recipe | Standard | Add 15–20% more dry straw to counter wet inputs |
| Straw pre-soak | 12–24 hours | Skip — straw already moist in monsoon |
| Cow dung storage | 3 days max | Use within 1 day or cover tightly |
| Risk | Anaerobic conditions, leachate runoff | Check drainage daily; turn immediately if rotten egg smell |

### Post-Monsoon / Rabi (October–November)

- Paddy straw harvest season — **buy and stockpile 3 months supply now** (cheapest at ₹1.5–2/kg post-harvest)
- Wet straw from fields: pre-dry for 3–5 days before shredding
- Decomposition rate increases as humidity stays moderate — good batches

### Winter (December–February) — Nights 12–15°C

| Parameter | Standard | Winter Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Pile temperature | 55–65°C target | May plateau at 45–55°C — acceptable |
| Turning | Every 7 days | Every 8–10 days (pile cools slowly) |
| Batch cycle | 11–12 weeks | Extend to 13–14 weeks |
| Insulation | Not needed | Optional: add 5 cm dry straw blanket to windrow sides |
| Recipe tweak | Standard | Increase N fraction by 5% (more dung) to boost microbial activity |

## 8. Troubleshooting Quick-Reference

### Problem Decision Tree

```
PROBLEM OBSERVED
      │
      ├── BAD SMELL?
      │     ├── Ammonia / Urine smell
      │     │     Cause:  Too much N, too wet, not turned
      │     │     Fix:    Add 100–150 kg dry straw, turn pile
      │     │             If persists after 2 days: turn again
      │     │
      │     └── Rotten egg / Sulphur smell
      │           Cause:  Anaerobic (waterlogged or compacted)
      │           Fix:    TURN IMMEDIATELY, add 200 kg dry straw
      │                   Improve drainage channel around pile
      │                   Check: is pile on flat ground with no runoff?
      │
      ├── TEMPERATURE PROBLEM?
      │     ├── Too cold (<40°C in Week 2–5)
      │     │     Cause A: Too dry → Fix: Water pile, 150 L into probe holes
      │     │     Cause B: Too much C (C:N too high) → Fix: Add 50–80 kg dung
      │     │     Cause C: Pile too small → Fix: Combine with adjacent windrow
      │     │     Cause D: Cold weather (winter) → Fix: Add straw insulation layer
      │     │
      │     └── Too hot (>72°C)
      │           Cause:  Dense pile, summer heat, excess N
      │           Fix:    TURN IMMEDIATELY, water edges during turning
      │                   Reduce pile height to 1.2 m after turn
      │                   Monitor twice daily until stabilised at 55–65°C
      │
      ├── MOISTURE PROBLEM?
      │     ├── Too dry (squeeze test: dusty, no moisture)
      │     │     Fix:    Probe 4–5 holes top to bottom with metal rod
      │     │             Add 20–30 L per hole slowly
      │     │             Or: do a full turn + water each layer
      │     │
      │     └── Too wet (water streams from fist)
      │           Fix:    Do not water. Turn pile, mixing in 200 kg dry straw
      │                   If monsoon: cover tightly with tarpaulin
      │                   Dig or clear drainage channel around pile
      │
      ├── PEST PROBLEM?
      │     ├── Rodents / Rats (burrows visible)
      │     │     Fix:    Remove or deeply bury kitchen waste (>40 cm deep)
      │     │             Place wire mesh (hardware cloth) on ground under pile
      │     │             Set snap traps along site perimeter
      │     │             Never use poison baits near compost
      │     │
      │     └── Flies / Maggots on surface
      │           Fix:    Bury food waste in centre of pile immediately
      │                   Cover exposed surface with 5 cm soil
      │                   Turn pile — hot centre (55°C+) kills maggots
      │                   Avoid adding cooked food, meat, dairy
      │
      ├── SLOW DECOMPOSITION?
      │     Symptoms: Volume barely reduced after 4 weeks, pile cold
      │     Check:    1. Moisture (most common cause)
      │               2. C:N ratio (is there enough N?)
      │               3. Pile size (minimum 1 m³ to retain heat)
      │               4. Aeration (is base layer present?)
      │     Fix:      Add 100 kg cow dung slurry + 50 L water
      │               Turn pile, rebuild with fresh aeration base
      │               Add ½ kg (500 g) old compost as microbial inoculant
      │
      ├── WEED SEEDS SPROUTING IN FINISHED COMPOST?
      │     Cause:  Pile did not reach 55°C for 3+ consecutive days
      │     Fix (prevention): Ensure turning moves outer material to centre
      │                       every turn — outer material is cooler
      │     Fix (if already screened): Extend curing 2 more weeks
      │                                with thin pile and sun exposure
      │
      └── COMPOST FAILS GERMINATION TEST?
            Cause A: Immature — phytotoxic organic acids still present
                     Fix: Cure 2 more weeks, retest
            Cause B: Too saline (excess poultry litter)
                     Fix: Reduce poultry litter to <10% in next batch
            Cause C: Copper/zinc contamination from poultry litter
                     Fix: Source poultry litter from farms not using
                          copper/zinc growth promoters (ask supplier)
```

### Smell-to-Action Quick Table

| Smell | What It Means | Immediate Action |
|---|---|---|
| Earthy, forest-like | Healthy, good decomposition | Nothing — this is correct |
| Sweet, slightly fruity | Early fermentation (first 5–7 days) | Normal, no action |
| Ammonia / urine | Too much N, needs C or aeration | Add straw, turn |
| Rotten eggs (H₂S) | Anaerobic, waterlogged | Turn NOW, add dry material |
| Musty/mouldy (white fuzz visible) | Fungal growth (actinomycetes) | Normal and healthy — no action |
| Burning / scorched | Pile overheating, dry hot spot | Turn, water, reduce pile height |
| Chemical / solvent | Contaminated input | Identify source, isolate batch, do not use until tested |

## 9. Emergency Procedures

### 9.1 Pile Fire (Rare but Possible in Peak Summer)

Cause: Excessively dry pile (moisture <25%) + organic dust + heat

**Immediate steps:**
1. Do not attempt to smother with soil — this removes oxygen temporarily but fire can restart
2. Flood the pile with water from all sides (use full hose flow, not sprinkle)
3. Break up and turn the pile while flooding — spread material out flat
4. Check for hot spots using temperature probe after spreading
5. Once cool: rebuild with higher moisture content (target 60%)
6. Report: note date, windrow number, estimated material loss

**Prevention:** In April–May, check moisture daily. Never allow active windrows to drop below 40% moisture.

---

### 9.2 Monsoon Flooding of Site

**If water enters active composting zone:**
1. Open drainage channels — remove any blockage immediately
2. If windrow is inundated (>10 cm standing water): use pump to remove standing water
3. Turn affected windrow: add 300+ kg dry straw during turning to absorb excess moisture
4. Monitor for anaerobic smell (rotten egg) over next 48 hours — turn again if detected
5. Delay new windrow construction until water subsides

**Prevention:** Clear drainage channels monthly and immediately after each heavy rain event.

---

### 9.3 Shredder Breakdown

**Immediate:**
1. Switch off and disconnect power before inspecting
2. Check: blade blockage, broken belt, blown fuse
3. If blade blockage: use metal rod to clear jam, never use hands inside chute
4. If belt broken: keep spare belt on-site (cost ~₹800–1,200, order 2 spare belts at startup)

**If shredder is out for >3 days:**
- Use hand-chopping (kudali/sickle) for small batches
- Delay new windrow construction by 2–3 days
- Prioritise soaking materials longer to compensate for larger particle size

---

### 9.4 Operator Absence (Sick Leave)

**If Operator 1 absent:**
- Operator 2 does morning temperature round (simplified: centre probe only, flag anything <45°C or >70°C)
- Inform owner/manager immediately
- Postpone windrow construction by 1 day if both operators needed

**If both operators absent (emergency):**
- Owner/manager does temperature round and moisture check
- Windrows already at correct moisture and temperature can go 2–3 days without intervention
- Do not start new windrow without both operators present
- Contact KVK or casual labour if absence extends >3 days
