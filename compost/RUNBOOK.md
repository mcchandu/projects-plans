```
NEW WINDROW      Every 3 days | 4,200 kg input | 5m × 2.5m × 1.5m
MOISTURE         50–60% active | 30–40% in storage
TEMPERATURE      55–65°C during weeks 2–4
TURNING          Every 5–7 days (active phase)
BATCH CYCLE      11–12 weeks from build to finished compost

SQUEEZE TEST
  Water drips freely   = Too wet  → Add dry straw
  1–2 drops appear     = CORRECT
  Moist, no drops      = Slightly dry → Water soon
  Dry, dusty           = Too dry  → Water now

TEMPERATURE GUIDE
  55–70°C  = Good, healthy decomposition
  <40°C (Week 2–4) = Problem — see Troubleshooting
  >70°C    = Turn immediately

SMELL GUIDE
  Earthy / forest smell  = Good
  Ammonia / urine        = Add straw + turn
  Rotten egg             = Turn immediately
  Musty / white fuzz     = Normal (fungal growth)
```

## SOPs

### SOP-01: Receiving Raw Materials

**Who:** Operator 2 | **Time:** 20–30 min per delivery

1. Direct the vehicle to the receiving area.
2. Weigh each material type.
3. Write in the Raw Material Register: date, supplier, material, weight, condition.
4. Sort materials:
   - Dry browns (straw, leaves) → shredding zone
   - Wet greens (dung, litter) → shaded corner, use within 3 days
   - Neem cake → covered store, use within 7 days
5. **Reject** any material with plastic, dead animals, oil smell, or diseased plants.

---

### SOP-02: Shredding & Pre-Soaking

**Who:** Operator 2 | **Time:** 3.5–4 hours per windrow batch

1. Check shredder before starting — blades, belt, discharge chute. Wear eye protection and gloves.
2. Feed dry straw in small bundles. Target output size: **5–8 cm**.
3. Collect shredded material in a pile.
4. Fill the soak pit with 400–500 L water + 50 kg cow dung (make a slurry).
5. Pour slurry over shredded straw. Soak overnight (8–24 hours).
6. Next morning: check soaked straw with squeeze test — moist but not dripping.

**Daily target:** 700 kg dry material shredded (about 3.5 hours)

---

### SOP-03: Building a Windrow

**Who:** Operator 1 + Operator 2 | **Time:** 2.5–3 hours

**Materials per windrow (4,200 kg total):**

| Material | Amount |
|---|---|
| Pre-soaked straw | 1,764 kg (42%) |
| Cow dung | 1,260 kg (30%) |
| Dry leaves | 588 kg (14%) |
| Neem cake | 336 kg (8%) |
| Poultry litter | 168 kg (4%) |
| Press mud | 84 kg (2%) |

**Steps:**
1. Mark footprint on ground: 5 m long × 2.5 m wide.
2. Build in layers:

| Layer | Material | Thickness |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (base) | Thick stalks | 10 cm |
| 2 | Soaked straw | 20 cm |
| 3 | Cow dung + poultry litter | 10 cm |
| 4 | Leaves + neem cake | 15 cm |
| 5 | Soaked straw | 15 cm |
| 6 | Cow dung + press mud | 10 cm |
| 7 (top) | Thin soil or old compost | 3 cm |

3. Shape into a trapezoid: 2.5 m base, 0.8 m top, 1.5 m tall.
4. Check moisture at 3 spots. Sprinkle water on any dry patches.
5. Insert a numbered stake with the build date.
6. Record in Windrow Register.
7. Check temperature after 48 hours — should be rising.

---

### SOP-04: Daily Monitoring

**Who:** Operator 1 | **Time:** 45–60 min | **When:** Every morning, 8–9 AM

1. Walk all active windrows in the same order every day.
2. For each windrow, record:
   - Temperature at 3 spots (left, centre, right — probe 40 cm deep)
   - Smell (earthy = good / ammonia = problem / rotten egg = urgent)
   - Moisture (squeeze test on outer edge)
3. Flag windrows:
   - 🔴 Red = Turn today
   - 🟡 Yellow = Add water today
   - 🟢 Green = Fine, check tomorrow
4. Tell the owner/manager if any windrow is below 45°C during Weeks 2–5.

---

### SOP-05: Turning a Windrow

**Who:** Operator 1 + Operator 2 | **Time:** 1.5–2 hours per windrow

**When to turn:**

| Week | Turn Number | Day |
|---|---|---|
| Week 2 | Turn 1 | Day 10–12 |
| Week 3 | Turn 2 | Day 17–19 |
| Week 4 | Turn 3 | Day 24–26 |
| Week 5–6 | Turn 4 | Day 33–36 |
| Week 7–8 | Turn 5 | Day 45–50 |

**Steps:**
1. Clear 1 m of space beside the windrow.
2. Move outer/edge material to the centre of the new pile first — this is the key step (edges are cooler and less decomposed).
3. Build the new windrow alongside the old one, shifting 0.5 m sideways.
4. While turning, check moisture:
   - Dry edges → spray water as you layer
   - Wet slimy centre → add dry straw
5. Take temperature reading 24 hours after turning — a good turn reheats to 55°C+ within 1–2 days.
6. Record in Windrow Register: turn number, date, temperature before and after.

---

### SOP-06: Managing Moisture

**When:** Any time daily monitoring flags it

**Squeeze test guide:**

| Result | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Water drips freely | Too wet (>65%) | Add dry straw, turn soon |
| 1–2 drops | Correct (55–60%) | Nothing needed |
| Moist, no drops | Slightly dry (45–50%) | Water at next turn |
| Dry and dusty | Too dry (<40%) | Water immediately |

**How to water without turning:**
1. Poke 5–6 holes (5 cm wide, 60 cm deep) along the top of the windrow.
2. Insert hose and add 20–30 L per hole slowly.
3. Cover holes with loose compost.

**Summer rule (Mar–May):** Check moisture every day. Water every 1–2 days. Use shade net.

---

### SOP-07: Moving to Curing Zone

**Who:** Both operators | **Time:** 1–1.5 hours

**Check before moving:**
- [ ] Temperature ≤ 40°C and falling
- [ ] Smell is earthy — no ammonia, no rotten egg
- [ ] Volume reduced to ~35% of original
- [ ] Material looks dark and uniform

**Steps:**
1. Do one final turn to aerate.
2. Move material to the curing zone.
3. Build a lower, wider pile — max 0.8 m tall.
4. Tag with windrow number and transfer date.
5. Cover loosely with shade net.
6. Record in Windrow Register.

---

### SOP-08: Maturity Testing

**Who:** Operator 1 | **When:** Week 10–11 (2–3 weeks after moving to curing)

**All 5 tests must pass before dispatching.**

| Test | How | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Temperature | Insert probe 30 cm deep | Within 5°C of outside air |
| 2. Smell | Take a handful, smell it | Earthy, like forest floor |
| 3. Visual | Spread on white paper | Dark brown, crumbly, no visible straw or dung |
| 4. Bag test | Seal 250 g in bag, leave 2 days in sun, open and smell | Earthy, no bad smell |
| 5. Germination | Plant 10 radish seeds in compost + 10 in soil. Compare Day 7. | Compost pot ≥ 80% of soil pot germination |

If any test fails → cure 1–2 more weeks and retest.

---

### SOP-09: Screening

**Who:** Operator 2 | **Time:** 2.5–3 hours per ~3,500 kg

1. Set up the sieve (6–8 mm mesh) on flat ground.
2. Shovel cured compost onto the sieve in small amounts.
3. Fine material (passes through) → storage bay.
4. Coarse rejects (stay on top) → add to the next fresh windrow (don't throw away).
5. Weigh screened output and record.
6. Move screened compost to covered storage immediately.

> Normal reject rate: 8–12%. If >15%, sharpen or adjust the shredder.

---

### SOP-10: Bagging & Dispatch

**Who:** Operator 2 | **Time:** 1–1.5 hours per ton

1. Fill bags to slightly over target weight (50 kg bags → fill to 51–52 kg).
2. Check moisture before bagging — should be 30–35%. If dusty, lightly mist. If too wet, spread in sun for 30 minutes.
3. Seal bags tightly.
4. Label every bag:
   - Product name: "Organic Compost"
   - Net weight
   - Batch number (format: YYYYMM-01, e.g., 202604-01)
   - Your farm name + address
   - Date made + best before (6 months)
   - NPK content (from lab test)
5. Stack on pallets or raised wooden planks — never directly on wet ground.
6. Write a delivery note for every order: buyer name, date, bag count, weight, rate, amount.
7. Record in Dispatch Register.

## Seasonal Adjustments

### Summer (March–May) — Up to 43°C in Hyderabad

| What | Normal | Summer Change |
|---|---|---|
| Watering | Every 3–4 days | Every 1–2 days |
| Shade net | Optional | Mandatory |
| Windrow height | 1.5 m | Reduce to 1.2 m |
| Turning | Every 7 days | Every 5 days |
| Work hours | 8 AM–5 PM | 6–11 AM, then 4–7 PM |

### Monsoon (June–September)

| What | Normal | Monsoon Change |
|---|---|---|
| Windrow cover | Shade net | Full tarpaulin, sloped to drain |
| Turning | Every 7 days | Every 10 days |
| Watering | As needed | Usually zero |
| Straw in recipe | Standard | Add 15–20% extra dry straw |
| Drain channels | Monthly check | Check after every heavy rain |

### Post-Monsoon (October–November)
- Best time to buy paddy straw — cheapest at ₹1.5–2/kg after harvest.
- Pre-dry wet straw for 3–5 days before shredding.

### Winter (December–February)
- Batch cycle may extend to 13–14 weeks (slower decomposition).
- Add 5 cm dry straw on the sides of windrows for insulation.
- Use slightly more dung (5% extra) to boost heat.

## Troubleshooting

### Problem → Cause → Fix

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Ammonia smell | Too much N, not turned | Add 100–150 kg dry straw, turn pile |
| Rotten egg smell | Waterlogged, no air | **Turn immediately**, add 200 kg dry straw, fix drainage |
| Too cold (<40°C, weeks 2–5) | Too dry / too much straw / pile too small | Add water + 50–80 kg dung; combine piles if small |
| Too hot (>72°C) | Dense pile, excess N, summer heat | Turn immediately, water edges, lower pile to 1.2 m |
| Too dry (dusty) | Hot weather, not watered | Poke holes, add 20–30 L water per hole |
| Too wet (water streams out) | Overwatered or monsoon rain | Add dry straw, turn, cover with tarpaulin |
| Slow decomposition | Low moisture or not enough N | Add dung slurry + 50 L water, turn and rebuild |
| Rats / rodents | Kitchen waste on surface | Bury food scraps deep (40+ cm), add wire mesh under pile |
| Flies / maggots | Exposed food waste | Bury food waste, cover surface with 5 cm soil, turn |
| Weed seeds in finished compost | Pile never hit 55°C | Ensure pile reaches 55–65°C; turn frequently |
| Fails germination test | Immature / too much poultry litter | Cure 2 more weeks; reduce poultry litter to <10% next batch |

### Smell Quick Guide

| Smell | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Earthy / forest | Healthy | Nothing |
| Sweet / fruity (first week) | Early fermentation | Normal, ignore |
| Ammonia / urine | Too much N or not turned | Add straw, turn |
| Rotten egg | Anaerobic | Turn now |
| White fuzzy mould | Fungal growth (actinomycetes) | Normal and healthy |
| Burning / scorched | Overheating, dry | Turn + water |
| Chemical smell | Contaminated input | Isolate batch, do not use until tested |

## Emergencies

### Pile Fire (rare — only in dry summer)
1. Flood with water from all sides using full hose.
2. Break up and spread the pile flat while flooding.
3. Check for hot spots with probe after spreading.
4. Rebuild with 60% moisture target.
5. **Prevention:** Never let active windrows drop below 40% moisture in summer.

### Monsoon Flooding
1. Open and clear all drainage channels immediately.
2. If water is inside the windrow zone — pump out standing water.
3. Turn affected windrow, add 300+ kg dry straw.
4. Watch for rotten egg smell over next 48 hours — turn again if detected.

### Shredder Breaks Down
1. Switch off and unplug before inspecting.
2. Check for blade blockage — clear with a metal rod, never use hands.
3. Check belt — keep 2 spare belts on site at all times (cost ₹800–1,200 each).
4. If down for 3+ days: hand-chop small batches, delay new windrow by 2–3 days.

### Operator Absent
- **One operator absent:** Other operator covers temperature round and basic checks. Delay windrow construction by 1 day.
- **Both absent:** Owner does temperature + moisture check. Windrows can survive 2–3 days without turning. Do not build new windrow without both operators.

## New Operator Training (5–7 Days)

### Day 1–2: Learn the Site
- [ ] Walk through all zones — understand each one's purpose
- [ ] Read the Quick Reference Card together out loud
- [ ] Learn the 5 stages of a windrow's life
- [ ] See all equipment and what each one does
- [ ] Safety rules: shredder, heat in summer, no chemicals

### Day 3–4: Do It With Supervisor
- [ ] Complete full morning monitoring round together (SOP-04)
- [ ] Squeeze test on 5–6 windrows — compare results and discuss
- [ ] Assist in a windrow turning (SOP-05)
- [ ] Assist in building a new windrow (SOP-03)
- [ ] Practice temperature probe — take readings at 3 depths

### Day 5–6: Do It Alone (Supervisor Checks)
- [ ] Operator completes morning round alone — supervisor checks results
- [ ] Operator fills out daily checklist alone
- [ ] Operator completes one shredding session alone (SOP-02)
- [ ] Operator practices maturity tests on a ready batch (SOP-08)

### Day 7: Sign-Off
- [ ] Can correctly read and act on 3 different smell situations
- [ ] Can tell the difference: pile needs water vs. pile needs turning
- [ ] Can fill the Windrow Register correctly
- [ ] Knows who to call in an emergency
