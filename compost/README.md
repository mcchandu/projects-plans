## 2. Site Selection & Layout

### 2.1 Choosing the Composting Area

**Must-have criteria:**

- **Distance from wells/water bodies:** Minimum **30 metres** from any open well, borewell, or water channel to prevent leachate contamination
- **Distance from residence/cattle shed:** At least **20–30 metres** downwind to manage odour
- **Drainage:** Slightly elevated ground (15–30 cm above surrounding area), or grade the site so runoff flows away from the pile — never towards a water body
- **Shade:** Partial shade is ideal (under trees or a simple shade net) — reduces moisture evaporation in summer, but don't place under trees that drop allelopathic leaves (eucalyptus, lantana)
- **Access road:** Must allow a tractor or loaded cart to reach the site year-round, including monsoon months
- **Sunlight:** Some direct sun helps kill pathogens during the active phase — avoid fully enclosed dark pits
- **Soil type:** Firm, compacted laterite/black cotton soil is fine — avoid waterlogged or low-lying spots

**Red flags to avoid:**
- Low-lying areas that flood during Kharif rains (June–October in Telangana)
- Proximity to vegetable crop fields (odour, potential contamination during maturation)
- Slope greater than 5° (leachate will run off before it can be managed)

### 4.2 C:N Ratio Basics

- **Target C:N ratio at pile setup: 25–30:1**
- Below 20:1 → Too much nitrogen → Ammonia smell, wet pile
- Above 35:1 → Too much carbon → Slow decomposition, pile stays cold
- Mix **"Greens" (N-rich, wet)** with **"Browns" (C-rich, dry)** to hit the sweet spot

**Simple thumb rule for field mixing:**
> For every **1 part cow dung** (by weight), add **3–4 parts paddy straw/dry matter**

## 5. Composting Method

### Recommended Methods for Your Situation

For a Telangana farm context, **two methods are recommended:**

1. **Windrow Composting** — for Scenarios B and C (5–10 tons/month)
2. **Pit/Trench Composting** — for Scenario A (1 ton/month) or monsoon season

Both are well-suited to the hot, semi-arid climate of Telangana with its two distinct seasons (hot dry + wet monsoon).

---

### 5.1 Method A: Windrow Composting (Primary Recommendation)

**Best for:** Scenarios B and C, open-air, good drainage site

#### Step-by-Step Process Flow

**Step 1 — Shredding & Pre-processing**
- Shred dry materials (straw, stalks, dry leaves) to **5–8 cm length**
- Shredding increases surface area → faster decomposition
- Pre-soak straw in water or cow dung slurry for 12–24 hours before stacking
- Do NOT shred cow dung or kitchen waste — add as-is

**Step 2 — Windrow Construction**
- Build windrow in alternating layers (lasagne style):

```
Layer 6: Thin soil/compost (2–3 cm)         ← Top cover
Layer 5: Green material (cow dung/poultry litter, 10 cm)
Layer 4: Brown material (straw/leaves, 15–20 cm)
Layer 3: Green material (10 cm)
Layer 2: Brown material (15–20 cm)
Layer 1 (base): Coarse brown material/stalks (5–10 cm) ← Aeration base
```

- **Windrow dimensions:**
  - Height: **1.2–1.5 m** (do not exceed 1.8 m — limits oxygen penetration)
  - Width: **1.5–2.0 m** at base
  - Length: As needed (each 6 m length ≈ 1–1.5 ton input material)
  - Keep each windrow as a separate batch for tracking

**Step 3 — Initial Moisture Setting**
- Target moisture: **50–60%** (squeeze test: water should barely drip through a tight fist)
- If too dry: water each layer during stacking
- In summer (Hyderabad can hit 42°C), you may need 100–150 L/ton extra water at setup
- If too wet (monsoon season): mix in extra dry straw or cover pile partially

**Step 4 — Active Decomposition Phase (Weeks 1–6)**

| Week | Action | Target Temperature | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Build pile, check moisture | 35–45°C (rising) | Temperature should rise within 3–5 days |
| Week 2 | First turning | 55–65°C (peak) | Move outer material to centre |
| Week 3 | Second turning | 50–60°C | Check moisture, add water if dry |
| Week 4 | Third turning | 45–55°C | Pile shrinks ~30–40% in volume |
| Week 5 | Fourth turning | 40–50°C | Smell should be earthy, not putrid |
| Week 6 | Fifth turning (if needed) | 35–40°C | Material darkens, structure breaks down |

- **Turning method:** Use a fork or front loader to flip the pile — move the outer edges to the centre and vice versa
- Each turning takes ~1–2 person-hours for a 6 m windrow
- In peak summer: turn every **5–6 days** to prevent overdrying and maintain biology
- In monsoon: turn every **7–10 days** — cover pile ends, maintain drainage

**Step 5 — Maturation/Curing Phase (Weeks 7–10)**
- Move compost to curing area (separate from active zone)
- Pile height: reduce to 0.8–1.0 m
- No regular turning needed — turn once or twice over 3–4 weeks
- Temperature should drop to near ambient (30–35°C in Telangana summer)
- Cover loosely with shade net

**Step 6 — Screening & Storage**
- Screen through 6–8 mm mesh sieve
- Oversized material (undecomposed wood chips, large stalks) goes back into the next batch
- Store finished compost in covered shed or under tarpaulin — do not leave exposed to direct rain (leaches nutrients) or direct sun (kills beneficial organisms)
- Shelf life: 6–8 months if stored dry

---

### 5.2 Method B: Pit/Trench Composting (Monsoon Season / Scenario A)

**Best for:** Small scale (Scenario A), monsoon months (June–October), when windrow turning is difficult

#### Setup
- Dig pits: **2 m × 1.5 m × 1 m deep** (each pit handles ~600–700 kg input → ~200 kg output)
- Space pits 1 m apart for access
- Line pit walls with mud plaster or polythene sheet (optional) to reduce moisture loss
- Raise a 15–20 cm earthen bund around pit opening to prevent rainwater flooding

#### Process
- Fill pit in layers (same recipe as windrow)
- Cover top with 5 cm soil + tarpaulin during heavy rain
- Turn by emptying pit and refilling into adjacent pit at 3-week intervals
- Total time: 8–12 weeks (slower than windrow due to less aeration)
- Good for kitchen waste, cow dung-heavy mixes

## 8. Quality, Maturity & Testing

### 8.1 What Good Finished Compost Looks, Smells, and Feels Like

| Attribute | Good Compost | Problem Indicator |
|---|---|---|
| **Colour** | Dark brown to black | Light brown/grey = immature; black + slimy = too wet |
| **Smell** | Earthy, forest-floor smell | Ammonia smell = too much N, not turned; Rotten egg smell = anaerobic, too wet |
| **Texture** | Crumbly, loose, like moist soil | Sticky/slimy = immature/wet; Dusty = over-dried |
| **Particle size** | 95%+ passes 10 mm sieve | Large chunks = needs more curing or screening |
| **Temperature** | Near ambient (≤ 5°C above ambient) | Still hot = not mature, wait longer |
| **Identifiable materials** | Almost none | Visible straw, dung chunks = immature |
| **Moisture** | 30–40% at storage | Clumps and sticks in hand but no water drips = good |

---

### 8.2 Simple On-Farm Maturity Tests

**Test 1 — Temperature Test (simplest)**
- Insert compost thermometer 30 cm into pile
- If temperature is within 5°C of ambient air temperature after turning: **mature**
- If it heats up again after turning: still active, wait 1–2 more weeks

**Test 2 — Smell Test**
- Take a handful, smell closely
- Earthy/soil-like smell = ready
- Any ammonia or rotten smell = needs more time

**Test 3 — Bag Test (Plastic Bag Seal Test)**
- Put 250 g compost in sealed plastic bag, leave in sun for 2 days
- Open and smell — if earthy and no foul odour: ready
- If foul or strong smell: needs more curing

**Test 4 — Seed Germination Test (best quality indicator)**
- Fill two small pots: one with 100% finished compost, one with regular garden soil
- Plant 10 radish or onion seeds in each
- After 5–7 days, compare germination rate
- If compost pot shows ≥ 80% germination compared to soil pot: **compost is mature and non-toxic**
- Low germination in compost pot = still contains phytotoxic compounds = needs more curing

**Test 5 — Visual Decomposition Check**
- Spread handful on white paper
- No identifiable straw, dung, or fresh material visible = mature
- Should look uniformly dark brown/black and granular

---

### 8.3 Basic Quality Parameters to Track

| Parameter | Target Value | How to Measure |
|---|---|---|
| Moisture content | 30–40% | Moisture meter or oven-dry method |
| pH | 6.5–7.5 | pH meter or strips (test in 1:5 water extract) |
| Particle size | <10 mm (90%+) | Screen through 10 mm sieve |
| C:N ratio (finished) | 12–18:1 | Lab test (send sample to soil testing lab) |
| Approximate N content | 1.5–2.5% (dry weight) | Lab test |
| Approximate P content | 0.5–1.5% (dry weight) | Lab test |
| Approximate K content | 1.0–2.0% (dry weight) | Lab test |
| Organic matter % | 25–35% | Lab test (send to ICAR/KVK lab) |

**Recommended:** Get one lab test done per crop season (₹500–1,500 at govt. soil testing labs or KVK Hyderabad) to understand your compost quality and adjust recipes.

## 9. Usage on Your Farmland

### 9.1 Recommended Application Rates

| Crop Type | Application Rate | Timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vegetables (brinjal, tomato, chilli, leafy greens)** | 5–8 tons/acre (1.2–2.0 kg/m²) | 2–3 weeks before transplanting | Mix into top 15 cm soil |
| **Paddy (rice)** | 3–5 tons/acre | Before puddling, work into soil | Reduces chemical N need by 25–30% |
| **Red gram / Tur dal** | 2–3 tons/acre | At land preparation | Legumes need less N but benefit from P and micronutrients |
| **Groundnut** | 3–5 tons/acre | At sowing | Improves pod yield and soil structure in red soils |
| **Maize / Jowar / Bajra** | 3–5 tons/acre | Before sowing, incorporated | Good return on investment for these crops |
| **Orchards (mango, guava, citrus)** | 15–25 kg/tree/year | Before monsoon onset (May–June) | Apply in ring around drip zone, 30 cm from trunk |
| **Fruit trees (young, 1–3 years)** | 5–10 kg/tree/year | Twice — pre-monsoon + post-Kharif | Critical establishment period |
| **Fodder crops** | 3–4 tons/acre | At planting | Improves biomass yield |

---

### 9.2 When to Apply

- **Best timing:** 2–3 weeks before sowing or transplanting — allows compost to integrate and microbes to establish
- **Monsoon application (June–July):** Incorporate into soil immediately to avoid nutrient leaching
- **Rabi season (Oct–Nov):** Apply and work in during land prep after Kharif harvest
- **For orchards:** Split application — half before monsoon (June), half after Dussehra (October)
- **Avoid:** Applying fresh/immature compost (will tie up soil nitrogen temporarily) or applying right at sowing (can burn tender roots)

---

### 9.3 Integrating Compost with Existing Fertilizer Practices

| Approach | How to Implement | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| **Substitution:** Replace 25–30% of DAP/Urea | Use compost at recommended rate + reduce chemical N by 1/4 to 1/3 | Cost saving + soil health improvement |
| **Integration:** Compost as base + chemicals as top-dress | Apply compost at land prep; apply urea/DAP at sowing and 30-day top-dress | Best agronomic outcome |
| **Soil health focus:** Full compost only (organic farming path) | Requires 2–3 seasons to build soil biology, yields may dip initially | For premium/organic market |
| **Fertigation integration** | Mix compost leachate (from collection pit) with drip water as liquid fertilizer | Low-cost, high efficiency for vegetable crops |

**Practical tip for Telangana black cotton soils (Vertisols):** These soils are naturally high in clay but low in organic matter. Even 2–3 tons/acre of compost significantly improves drainage, reduces cracking, and enhances root penetration. Prioritise these soils.

### 10.3 Compliance & Registration Overview (India)

> ⚠️ This is a high-level overview. Consult your local agricultural officer or a CA for specifics before commercial sale.

**1. Fertiliser Control Order (FCO), 1985**
- Compost sold commercially must comply with FCO standards for "Compost (city/municipal/rural)" or be registered as an organic fertilizer
- Key quality standards: Minimum 15% organic carbon, C:N ratio ≤ 20:1 for fully cured compost
- Registration: Apply to the State Department of Agriculture (Telangana Agriculture Dept.) for a Fertiliser Dealer License
- Cost: Nominal state fees (typically ₹1,000–5,000)
- Lab certification: Submit samples to a government-approved fertiliser testing lab

**2. APEDA / PGS-India (if selling as "Organic")**
- If marketing as certified organic input: consider PGS-India (Participatory Guarantee System) — a low-cost group certification scheme run by the Govt. of India
- Contact your local KVK (Krishi Vigyan Kendra) — there are KVKs in Ranga Reddy, Medchal, and Mahbubnagar districts
- Cost: Group-level participation, minimal individual cost

**3. GST**
- Compost (organic manure from agricultural waste) is **GST exempt** under HSN code 3101 (animal or vegetable fertilisers)
- No GST registration needed if annual turnover is under ₹40 lakhs
- For turnover above ₹40 lakhs: voluntary/mandatory GST registration (Composition scheme at 1%)

**4. Pollution Control**
- Composting of farm waste (agricultural residues, cow dung) is generally not regulated under PCB rules for small operations
- Avoid burning any material on-site and ensure no odour/drainage complaints from neighbours
- For a larger operation (above 10 tons/day), consult Telangana State Pollution Control Board (TSPCB)

**5. Trade License**
- Local Gram Panchayat/Municipality trade license if selling commercially: ₹500–2,000/year

## 11. Risks & Troubleshooting

### 11.1 Common Problems & Fixes

| Problem | Symptoms | Likely Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ammonia/bad smell** | Strong urine-like smell | Too much N (dung heavy), too wet, not turned | Add dry straw/leaves, turn pile, improve aeration |
| **Rotten egg smell (H₂S)** | Sulphur/sewage odour | Anaerobic conditions (waterlogged) | Turn immediately, add coarse dry material, improve drainage |
| **Pile not heating up** | Temperature stays below 40°C | Too dry, C:N too high (too much straw), pile too small | Add moisture + N source (dung/poultry), rebuild pile larger |
| **Pile overheating (>70°C)** | Steam rising, edges dry out | Dense pile, too much N, summer heat | Turn immediately, water edges, check for fire risk |
| **Too dry, dusty pile** | Crumbly, not decomposing | Low moisture, hot dry weather (Telangana summer) | Water pile thoroughly, cover with shade net |
| **Too wet, slimy pile** | Soggy, clumping, anaerobic smell | Over-watering, monsoon rain infiltration | Add dry straw/carbon, turn, improve cover |
| **Slow decomposition** | Little volume reduction after 4+ weeks | C:N too high, low moisture, or no activator | Add cow dung slurry as activator, check moisture, re-layer |
| **Rodent/rat infestation** | Burrows, nesting material | Kitchen waste/food scraps near surface | Bury food waste in centre of pile, avoid cooked food, use wire mesh base |
| **Fly/maggot problem** | Housefly swarms near pile | Food waste exposed, wet surface | Cover food waste with soil, turn pile, avoid meat/cooked food |
| **Weed germination in compost** | Seedlings in finished compost | Insufficient heating (didn't hit 55°C for 3+ days) | Ensure pile reaches 55–65°C, turn frequently, check thermometer |

---

### 11.2 Preventing Pathogens and Weed Seeds

**The key rule:** Pile must reach **55–65°C for at least 3 consecutive days** — this kills most weed seeds and pathogens (Salmonella, E. coli, etc.)

Practical steps to guarantee pathogen kill:
- Turn pile every 5–7 days to move outer (cooler) material to the hot centre
- Monitor with a stem thermometer — do not guess
- If using poultry litter or human-adjacent waste (canteen kitchen scraps), extend the active phase by 2 extra weeks
- After active phase, the curing phase further degrades any surviving organisms
- Never apply fresh/active (hot) compost to root vegetables (carrot, radish) — wait for full maturation

---

### 11.3 Telangana-Specific Seasonal Risks

| Season | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Summer (Mar–May)** | Pile dries out rapidly, biology slows, fire risk if too dry | Increase watering frequency, use shade net, monitor temperature daily |
| **Monsoon (Jun–Sep)** | Flooding, anaerobic conditions, leachate runoff | Use pit method or cover windrows, ensure drainage channels clear, reduce watering |
| **Post-monsoon (Oct–Nov)** | Excess moisture in raw materials (wet paddy straw) | Pre-dry straw for 3–5 days before use, add extra dry material in recipe |
| **Winter (Dec–Feb)** | Decomposition slows (cooler nights, 12–15°C) | Use insulating cover (thick straw layer on pile sides), increase C:N ratio slightly lower (use more N) |

### 11.1 The Core Math: From Raw Input to Finished Output

**Conversion chain:**

```
Raw input (wet weight)
    → Active composting (loses ~40–50% moisture + CO₂ over 6–8 weeks)
    → Curing (loses another 10–15%)
    → Screening (rejects ~8–12% as oversize returns)
    → Finished compost (dry-ish, screened)

Rule of thumb:  1 kg finished compost requires ~3.2–3.5 kg raw input (wet weight)
```

**Working backwards from 10 tons/month output:**

| Step | Calculation | Quantity |
|---|---|---|
| Finished compost target | — | **10,000 kg/month** |
| Pre-screening compost needed (add 10% for rejects) | 10,000 ÷ 0.90 | ~11,100 kg |
| Raw input needed (×3.3 conversion factor) | 11,100 × 3.3 | **~36,600 kg/month (~36.6 tons)** |
| Input per day (26 working days) | 36,600 ÷ 26 | **~1,400 kg/day** |
| Input per new windrow batch (built every 3 days) | 1,400 × 3 | **~4,200 kg per windrow** |
| Number of windrows in active rotation | 8 weeks active ÷ 1 new windrow per 3 days | **~18 windrows at any time** |

### 11.2 Windrow Sizing — The Physical Dimensions

**Single windrow specification for a 4,200 kg input batch:**

```
Target pile mass:   4,200 kg input (wet)
Bulk density of mixed input (straw + dung + leaves):  ~350–400 kg/m³

Volume needed:  4,200 ÷ 375 = ~11.2 m³

Windrow cross-section (trapezoid):
  Base width:  2.5 m
  Top width:   0.8 m (natural angle of repose)
  Height:      1.5 m
  Cross-section area = ½ × (2.5 + 0.8) × 1.5 = 2.475 m²

Length needed:  11.2 m³ ÷ 2.475 m² = ~4.5 m  → build to 5 m (with working clearance)
```

**Single windrow footprint:**

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Base width | 2.5 m |
| Height | 1.5 m |
| Length | 5.0 m |
| Top width | ~0.8 m |
| Ground footprint (with 0.5 m access strip each side) | 2.5 m × 5.0 m = **12.5 m²** gross |

**What happens to the windrow as it decomposes:**

| Week | Volume (m³) | Height | Mass (kg, wet) | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | 11.2 | 1.5 m | 4,200 | Build |
| Week 2 | 9.5 | 1.35 m | 3,500 | Turn 1 |
| Week 3 | 8.2 | 1.25 m | 3,000 | Turn 2 |

> You need **8–9 windrows completing per month** → 1 new windrow built every **3–4 days**

### 11.3 Batch Rotation Model

**The 18-Windrow Rotating System:**

At steady state, your site holds windrows in 4 stages simultaneously:

| Zone | Stage | No. of Windrows | Weeks in Stage | Area Used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Active Zone A | Fresh → Hot decomposition (Wk 1–4) | 8 | 4 weeks | ~200 m² |
| Active Zone B | Maturing decomposition (Wk 5–8) | 6 | 4 weeks | ~140 m² |
| Curing Zone | Curing + stabilisation (Wk 9–11) | 4 | 3 weeks | ~80 m² |
| Screening/Storage | Finished product hold | — | ongoing | ~80 m² |
| **Total** | | **18 windrows** | | **~500–550 m² active** |

**New windrow construction schedule:**

```
Week 1, Day 1:   Build Windrow #1
Week 1, Day 4:   Build Windrow #2
Week 1, Day 7:   Build Windrow #3
...
Every 3 days, one new windrow is started
Every 3 days, one windrow completes curing and moves to screening
```

This creates a **continuous flow** — you are always producing, always screening.

#### D. Revenue Model & Break-Even

| Sales Channel | Volume | Price/kg | Monthly Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bulk farm gate (loose, per ton) | 4,000 kg | ₹5.00/kg | ₹20,000 |
| Bagged (50 kg PP bags, local farmers) | 4,000 kg | ₹8.00/kg | ₹32,000 |
| Bagged (10 kg branded, nurseries/urban) | 2,000 kg | ₹14.00/kg | ₹28,000 |
| **Total revenue** | **10,000 kg** | **blended ₹8.00/kg** | **₹80,000** |

**At this price mix:**
```
Revenue:          ₹80,000
OpEx:           - ₹1,28,000
Monthly loss:   - ₹48,000   ← This is the gap to close
```

**Break-even price needed:** ₹12.80/kg blended average

**Path to profitability:**

| Lever | Impact |
|---|---|
| Sell 30% at ₹14–18/kg (branded nursery/urban bags) | Adds ₹15,000–18,000/month revenue |
| Cut raw material cost by 20% (own farm residues, free dung) | Saves ₹13,500/month |
| Add vermicompost premium product (10% of output) | 2× price, higher margin |
| Reach 15 tons/month (marginal cost drops sharply) | Fixed costs spread over more output |
| Apply 3 tons/month on own farm (replace ₹25,000 in fertilizer) | ₹25,000 effective saving per month |

**Realistic break-even:** Month 6–9 when own-farm raw material supply is organised and a retail channel (nurseries, organic vegetable farmers) is established.
