## 1. How Big Should You Start?

| | **Starter** | **Small Farm** | **Commercial** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Output/month | 1 ton | 5 tons | 10 tons |
| Land needed | 150–200 m² | 500–700 m² | 1,000–1,200 m² |
| Workers | 1 part-time | 1–2 full-time | 3–4 full-time |
| Monthly cost | ₹5,000–8,000 | ₹20,000–30,000 | ₹40,000–60,000 |

> **Rule:** For every 3 kg of raw material you put in, you get about 1 kg of finished compost.

**Start with Starter size for 3–4 months. Learn the process. Then grow.**

---

## 2. Choosing Your Site

**You need:**
- 30 m away from wells or water sources
- 20–30 m away from your house (for smell)
- Slightly raised ground so water drains away
- A path that a tractor or cart can reach

**Avoid:**
- Low spots that flood in monsoon (June–October)
- Near vegetable fields
- Slopes steeper than 5°

## 4. What Materials to Use

### Common Materials Near Hyderabad

| Material | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cow dung | Green (N-rich) | Best activator, free or cheap from dairy |
| Paddy straw | Brown (C-rich) | Shred to 5–8 cm, soak before using |
| Dry leaves | Brown | Free from farm trees |
| Poultry litter | Green | Very strong N — use max 10–15% only |
| Neem cake | Green | Also keeps pests away |
| Sugarcane press mud | Green | From sugar mills |
| Groundnut shells | Brown | Shred well — slow to break down |
| Kitchen veg waste | Green | Include if available, no cooked food or meat |
| Wood ash | Neutral | Use very little (1–2%) — raises pH |

---

### The Mix Ratio

**Target C:N ratio = 25–30:1**

Simple rule:
> **1 part cow dung + 3–4 parts dry straw** = good mix

## 5. How to Make Compost

### Method 1 — Windrow (for 5–10 tons/month)

**Step 1 — Shred dry material**
- Cut straw and stalks to 5–8 cm pieces
- Soak in water or dung slurry for 12–24 hours before using

**Step 2 — Build the pile in layers**
```
Top:    Thin soil or old compost (3 cm)
        Cow dung layer (10 cm)
        Dry straw layer (15 cm)
        Cow dung layer (10 cm)
        Dry straw layer (20 cm)
Base:   Thick stalks for air flow (10 cm)
```
- Height: 1.2–1.5 m | Width: 1.5–2 m | Length: as needed

**Step 3 — Set moisture**
- Target: 50–60% wet
- Squeeze test: squeeze a handful — 1–2 drops of water = correct

**Step 4 — Turn and watch for 6 weeks**

| Week | Action | Temperature |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build pile | Should rise to 35–45°C |
| 2 | Turn #1 | 55–65°C (hot phase) |
| 3 | Turn #2 | 50–60°C |
| 4 | Turn #3 | 45–55°C |
| 5 | Turn #4 | 40–50°C |
| 6 | Turn #5 if needed | 35–40°C |

**Step 5 — Cure for 3–4 weeks**
- Move to curing area, lower the pile height to 0.8–1 m
- Turn once or twice only

**Step 6 — Screen and store**
- Sieve through 6–8 mm mesh
- Store under cover — lasts 6–8 months

---

### Method 2 — Pit Composting (for 1 ton/month or monsoon season)

- Dig pits: 2 m × 1.5 m × 1 m deep
- Fill in layers (same as windrow)
- Cover with tarpaulin during heavy rain
- Turn every 3 weeks by moving to the next pit
- Ready in 12–14 weeks

## 7. Time and Work Needed

**Full batch time: 10–12 weeks**

```
Week 1–2:  Collect and shred materials, build pile
Week 2–6:  Turn pile every 5–7 days
Week 7–10: Curing (leave it mostly alone)
Week 10–11: Screen and store
```

**Work per ton of finished compost: ~20–30 person-hours**

## 8. How to Know It's Ready

### Signs of Good Compost
| What | Good | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Colour | Dark brown/black | Light = not ready |
| Smell | Earthy, like forest floor | Ammonia = bad; Rotten egg = very bad |
| Texture | Crumbly, like soil | Slimy = too wet; Dusty = too dry |
| Temperature | Same as outside air | Still hot = not ready |

### 5 Simple Tests

1. **Temperature** — insert thermometer 30 cm deep. Should be within 5°C of outside air.
2. **Smell** — take a handful and smell. Should be earthy, not bad.
3. **Bag test** — seal 250 g in a plastic bag for 2 days. Open and smell. Should be earthy.
4. **Germination test** — plant 10 radish seeds in compost, 10 in regular soil. After 5–7 days, compost should have at least 80% as many sprouted seeds as the soil.
5. **Visual check** — spread on white paper. Should look uniform, dark, no visible straw or dung.

## 9. How Much to Apply on Crops

| Crop | Amount | When |
|---|---|---|
| Vegetables | 5–8 tons/acre | 2–3 weeks before planting |
| Rice (paddy) | 3–5 tons/acre | Before puddling |
| Groundnut | 3–5 tons/acre | At sowing |
| Maize/Jowar | 3–5 tons/acre | Before sowing |
| Mango/fruit trees | 15–25 kg/tree/year | Before monsoon (May–June) |

> Mix into top 15 cm of soil. Never apply fresh/unfinished compost — it can damage roots.

## 11. Problems and Fixes

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Ammonia smell | Too much dung, not turned | Add dry straw, turn the pile |
| Rotten egg smell | Waterlogged, no air | Turn immediately, add dry material |
| Pile not heating | Too dry, or C:N wrong | Add water + dung, rebuild |
| Too hot (>70°C) | Dense pile, summer | Turn immediately, water edges |
| Too dry | Hot weather | Water daily, use shade net |
| Too wet | Monsoon rain | Add straw, improve drainage cover |
| Slow decomposition | Low moisture or N | Add dung slurry + water |
| Rats | Kitchen waste on surface | Bury food scraps deep in pile |
| Flies/maggots | Food waste exposed | Cover with soil, turn pile |
| Weed seeds in compost | Pile never got hot enough | Make sure pile hits 55–65°C next time |

### Seasonal Risks (Telangana)
| Season | Risk | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Summer (Mar–May) | Pile dries fast | Water every 1–2 days, use shade net |
| Monsoon (Jun–Sep) | Flooding, too wet | Cover windrows, keep drains clear |
| Post-monsoon (Oct–Nov) | Wet straw from fields | Pre-dry straw 3–5 days before using |
| Winter (Dec–Feb) | Slow decomposition | Add straw insulation around pile sides |
