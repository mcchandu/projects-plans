## 1. Worm Species to Use

### Best Choice — *Eisenia fetida* (Red Wigglers)

| Property | Detail |
|---|---|
| Common name | Red wigglers / red earthworms |
| Telugu name | Erra vennu puru |
| Why best | Eats fast, reproduces fast, surface feeder — perfect for beds |
| Ideal temperature | 20–30°C |
| Dies above | 38°C — **covered shed is mandatory in Hyderabad** |
| Population doubles | Every 60–90 days |
| Starting quantity | 1–2 kg per bed |

### Where to Buy Worms (Hyderabad)

| Source | Notes |
|---|---|
| ICAR-CRIDA Hyderabad (Santoshnagar) | Visit or call — sell starter cultures |
| KVK Ranga Reddy / Medchal | Often free or subsidised for farmers |
| IndiaMART / Amazon | ₹200–400/kg, shipped in moist newspaper |

**Cost:** ₹200–500/kg. Need 1–2 kg per bed. Worms multiply — buy once.

## 5. Bed Cross-Section — Layer by Layer

Fill each bed in this order from bottom to top:

| Layer | Material | Thickness | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (base) | Gravel or coarse stones (optional) | 5–8 cm | Drainage |
| 2 | Dry leaves, sticks, coarse stalks | 8–10 cm | Air flow and drainage |
| 3 | Partially decomposed material (15–20 day pre-compost) | 20–25 cm | Worm habitat and food |
| 4 (top) | Cooled compost material | 15–20 cm | Active feeding zone |
| Cover | Wet gunny sack or shade cloth | — | Keep dark and moist |

> **Critical:** Material must be pre-composted 15–20 days and cooled to below 30°C before adding worms. Fresh or hot material will kill worms.

## 6. Pre-Composting Step (Zone 3) — Why It Matters

This is the step most beginners skip — and it's why their worms die.

**What it is:** Before material goes into the worm beds, you windrow-compost it for 15–20 days to:
- Cool down the material (fresh dung can hit 50–60°C — kills worms)
- Remove ammonia from fresh cow dung
- Partially break down tough fibres so worms can eat easily
- Kill weed seeds and pathogens

**How to do it:**
1. Mix raw materials in Zone 2 (shred, set C:N ratio)
2. Build small windrows in Zone 3 — height 0.8–1 m, width 1.5 m
3. Water to 50–60% moisture
4. Turn once after 7–10 days
5. After 15–20 days — check temperature: must be **below 30°C** before loading into beds
6. Check smell: earthy, no strong ammonia = ready for worms

**This step takes 15–20 days but saves your entire worm population.**

## 7. Setting Up the Covered Shed (Zone 4)

### Shade Structure Requirements

| Item | Specification |
|---|---|
| Roof type | GI sheet or 75% shade net |
| Size | 30m × 20m (covers all 20 beds) |
| Height | Minimum 2.5 m (working clearance) |
| Sides | Open or 50% shade net — allow airflow |
| Floor | Compacted soil or concrete with slight slope for drainage |
| Base of beds | Wire mesh (6 mm) to block moles and rats |

**Shade is not optional in Hyderabad** — direct sun raises bed temperature above 38°C and kills worms.

### Bed Arrangement in Shed

```
  ← 30 m →
┌──────────────────────────────┐
│ Bed 1   Bed 2  ... Bed 10   │  Row A
│─────── 1.5 m path ──────────│
│ Bed 11  Bed 12 ... Bed 20   │  Row B
└──────────────────────────────┘
  ↕ 20 m
```

Day 1–5:    Site prep, shed construction begins

## 12. Scale-Up — Worm Multiplication

You don't need to keep buying worms. Population doubles every 60–90 days.

| Month | Worm Population | Beds Supportable |
|---|---|---|
| Month 1 | Starter (40 kg) | 20 beds |
| Month 3 | ~80 kg | 20 beds, surplus to sell |
| Month 6 | ~160 kg | Expand to 40 beds OR sell 80 kg worms |
| Month 12 | Self-sustaining, split as needed | — |

**Split a bed when:** worms are very dense and crawling over each other, or top 10 cm is fully converted to castings.
