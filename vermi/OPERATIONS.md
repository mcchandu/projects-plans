## The 8-Step Process (Full Flow)

This is the complete journey from raw material to finished product.

```
① RAW MATERIAL        Collect cow dung, crop waste, kitchen scraps
   COLLECTION         Sort and weigh at Zone 1

        ↓

② SHREDDING &         Shred dry material to 5–8 cm
   MIXING             Mix greens + browns in right ratio (Zone 2)

        ↓

③ PRE-COMPOSTING      Build small windrows in Zone 3
   (15–20 days)       Let material heat up, cool down, lose ammonia
                      DO NOT skip this step — hot material kills worms

        ↓

④ FILL VERMI BEDS     Only when material is COOLED below 30°C
   (Zone 4)           Fill beds in layers, add worms on top

        ↓

⑤ VERMICOMPOSTING     Worms eat and digest the material
   (45–60 days)       Feed every 3–4 days, check moisture daily

        ↓

⑥ HARVESTING &        Scoop out finished vermicompost
   SCREENING          Sieve through 3–5 mm mesh (Zone 5)

        ↓

⑦ STORAGE &           Store dry in covered shed (Zone 6)
   BAGGING            Bag, weigh, label in Zone 7

        ↓

⑧ SALES /             Dispatch to customers or use on your own farm
   FARM USE
```

## Step 1 — Raw Material Collection

**Who:** Operator 2 | **When:** As materials arrive

### What to Collect

| Material | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cow dung | Green (N-rich) | Must be pre-composted 15–20 days — never use fresh |
| Crop residues (straw, stalks) | Brown (C-rich) | Shred to 5–8 cm before use |
| Dry leaves | Brown | Free from farm — shred if possible |
| Vegetable / kitchen scraps | Green | Chop small (<5 cm), no cooked food |
| Coco peat / coir pith | Brown | Good bulking agent, holds moisture |
| Sugarcane press mud | Green | Rich activator — use max 10% |
| Biogas slurry | Green | Dilute 1:3 with water before use |

### What NOT to Collect

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| Fresh cow dung (not pre-composted) | Too hot (50–60°C), ammonia kills worms |
| Meat, fish, bones | Attracts rats, bad smell |
| Dairy products | Smell and pests |
| Oily / fried food | Suffocates worms |
| Citrus peel in large amounts | Too acidic |
| Onion / garlic in large amounts | Repels worms |
| Chemical-treated plant material | Kills worms immediately |
| Soil or clay | Too heavy, suffocates worms |

### Monthly Material Needed (20 beds)

| Material | Monthly Quantity |
|---|---|
| Pre-composted cow dung | 8,000–10,000 kg |
| Crop residues (shredded) | 4,000–5,000 kg |
| Coco peat / dry leaves | 2,000–3,000 kg |
| **Total input** | **~14,000–18,000 kg (14–18 tonnes)** |

## Step 2 — Shredding & Mixing (Zone 2)

**Who:** Operator 2 | **Time:** 3–4 hours/day

1. Feed dry material into shredder in small bundles.
2. Target output size: **5–8 cm pieces**.
3. Mix shredded dry material + wet green material together.
4. Target mix ratio: **60% greens + 40% browns** by weight.
5. Check moisture of mix — should be 50–60% (squeeze: moist but not dripping).
6. Move mixed material to Zone 3 for pre-composting.

---

## Step 3 — Pre-Composting (Zone 3) ⚠️ Do Not Skip

**Why:** Fresh material is too hot for worms. This step cools it down and removes ammonia.

**Time needed: 15–20 days**

### How to Pre-Compost

1. Build a small windrow with mixed material — 0.8–1 m tall, 1.5 m wide.
2. Set moisture to 50–60%.
3. Leave for 7–10 days — it will heat up to 45–60°C. This is normal.
4. Turn once at Day 7–10.
5. Wait until Day 15–20.
6. **Temperature check before loading into beds:**
   - Insert thermometer 30 cm deep
   - Must read **below 30°C** — only then load into worm beds
   - If still above 30°C — wait 2–3 more days
7. **Smell check:** earthy, no strong ammonia = ready. Ammonia smell = wait more.

### Quick Check — Is It Ready for Worms?

| Test | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | Below 30°C | Above 30°C → wait |
| Smell | Earthy, mild | Ammonia → wait |
| Texture | Dark, slightly broken down | Still looks fresh/bright → wait |

## Step 4 — Filling the Vermi Beds (Zone 4)

**Who:** Both operators | **Time:** 30–45 min per bed

### Bed Layer Order (bottom to top)

| Layer | Material | Thickness |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (base) | Coarse dry leaves, sticks | 8–10 cm |
| 2 | Partially decomposed material (from Zone 3) | 20–25 cm |
| 3 (top) | Cooled pre-compost (main feeding layer) | 15–20 cm |
| Cover | Wet gunny sack | — |

### Steps

1. Check bed base — wire mesh intact, drainage clear.
2. Add layers from bottom to top as per table above.
3. Do moisture check on top layer — squeeze test, 1–2 drops = correct.
4. Do temperature check — must be below 30°C.
5. Spread worms (1–2 kg) evenly across the surface.
6. Cover with wet gunny sack — worms burrow down within hours.
7. Tag the bed with: **Bed number + Date filled**.
8. Record in Bed Register.
9. Check after 48 hours — worms should have burrowed in (not crawling out = good).

---

## Step 5 — Vermicomposting (45–60 Days)

This is the main production phase. Your job is to keep worms healthy and fed.

### Feeding

**How often:** Every 3–4 days
**How much:** 15–20 kg per bed per week
**Total for 20 beds:** 300–400 kg feed per week

**How to feed:**
1. Pull back gunny sack from one side.
2. Check if previous feed is mostly eaten (dark, worked-over surface). If not — skip this feeding.
3. Add feed in a **5–8 cm layer only** — never thick.
4. Sprinkle water lightly if feed looks dry.
5. Cover back with gunny sack.

**Feeding rotation — makes harvesting easier:**
```
Week 1–3:  Feed LEFT half of bed only
Week 4–6:  Feed RIGHT half of bed only

Result by Week 6:
  LEFT half → finished vermicompost (worms moved to right side)
  RIGHT half → active worms feeding here

Harvest LEFT side → add fresh bedding → repeat
```

### Moisture Checks

Check every bed every morning.

| Squeeze Result | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Water drips freely | Too wet (>70%) | Add dry coco peat or leaves, no watering |
| 1–2 drops appear | Correct (60–70%) | Nothing needed |
| Moist, no drops | Slightly dry (50%) | Water lightly today |
| Dusty, falls apart | Too dry (<40%) | Water immediately |

**Summer (Mar–May):** Water beds every day. Mist the shade net too.
**Monsoon (Jun–Sep):** Cover beds with tarpaulin. Reduce or stop watering.

### Temperature Checks

Check 3–4 beds randomly every morning.

| Temperature | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 20–30°C | Correct | Nothing needed |
| 30–35°C | Getting warm | Increase watering, improve air flow |
| Above 35°C | Too hot — worms at risk | Water immediately, remove top feed layer, open sides of shed |
| Below 15°C (winter) | Too cold — worms slow | Cover beds with extra gunny sacks at night |

**Emergency cooling (if above 35°C):**
1. Remove top 5 cm of feed material.
2. Water surface generously.
3. Open shed sides for 30 minutes to ventilate.
4. Re-cover with wet gunny sack.

### Worm Health Checks

Check 2–3 beds every day by digging in 10 cm with your hand.

| What You See | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Pink/red worms moving actively | Healthy | Nothing |
| Worms near surface in morning | Normal — came up to feed | Nothing |
| Worms crawling out of bed | Bed too hot/wet/acidic or toxic feed | Find cause — see Troubleshooting |
| Worms clumped in corners | Problem in bed centre | Check temperature + remove last feed |
| Flat, sluggish, pale worms | Too dry, or toxic material added | Water bed, remove suspect feed |
| White cocoons (small capsules) | Worm eggs — excellent sign | Nothing — population is growing |
| White fluffy mould on feed | Fungal growth (actinomycetes) | Normal and healthy — worms eat it |

## Step 6 — Harvesting (Zone 5)

**When:** Every 45–60 days per bed
**Signs a bed is ready:**
- Bottom 15–20 cm is very dark, fine, like coffee grounds
- Almost no original material visible
- Very few worms in the bottom (they moved up toward fresh feed)
- Earthy smell, almost no odour

### Method 1 — Light Migration (Preferred)

1. Stop feeding the bed for **5–7 days**.
2. Place fresh feed on one side as bait.
3. Wait 2–3 days — worms move toward the food.
4. Scoop out the other side (mostly finished vermicompost, few worms).
5. Sieve through 3–5 mm mesh — any worms fall back into the bed.
6. Move screened vermicompost to Zone 6 (storage).

### Method 2 — Full Bed Harvest (When Splitting Beds)

1. Dump entire bed contents onto a tarpaulin in bright sunlight.
2. Worms move to the bottom away from light.
3. Scoop away top layer (finished compost) in stages — worms keep going down.
4. Collect worms from the bottom and return to a fresh bed.
5. Sieve all collected vermicompost through 3–5 mm mesh.

### After Every Harvest

1. Return worms to this bed or move to a new bed if splitting.
2. Add fresh bedding layers (same as Step 4).
3. Start feeding again after 2–3 days.
4. Record: date harvested, kg output, bed number.

### Expected Output per Bed

| Bed Size | Input | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 6m × 1m × 0.6m | 1–1.5 tonnes | **0.5–0.9 tonnes per cycle** |
| 20 beds together | 20–30 tonnes | **10–18 tonnes per cycle (45–60 days)** |
| Monthly (steady state) | — | **5–10 tonnes/month** |

---

## Step 7 — Screening (Zone 5)

1. Feed harvested material into rotary sieve (3–5 mm mesh).
2. Fine fraction (passes through) = finished vermicompost → move to Zone 6.
3. Coarse rejects (stays on top) = undigested material → return to worm beds as feed.
4. Weigh screened output and record in register.
5. Normal reject rate: 10–15%. Above 20% = worms need more time next cycle.

---

## Step 8 — Storage, Bagging & Dispatch (Zones 6 & 7)

### Storage (Zone 6)
- Store in covered, dry area — no direct rain or sun.
- Target moisture for storage: **30–40%** (not wet, not dusty).
- If too wet: spread in shade for 1 day to dry before bagging.
- Shelf life: **6 months** if stored dry.

### Bagging (Zone 7)
1. Fill bags to correct weight — use platform scale.
   - 5 kg bags → fill to 5.2 kg
   - 10 kg bags → fill to 10.3 kg
   - 25 kg bags → fill to 25.5 kg
2. Seal bags tightly.
3. Label every bag:
   - Product: "Organic Vermicompost"
   - Net weight
   - Batch number (format: YYYYMM-XX, e.g., 202604-01)
   - Farm name + address
   - Date made + best before (6 months)
   - NPK content (from lab test)
4. Stack on pallets or raised platform — never on wet ground.

### Dispatch
- Write a delivery challan for every order: buyer name, date, bags, weight, rate, amount.
- Record in Dispatch Register.

## Worm Tea — Free Bonus Product

Worm tea = liquid that drains from beds through the drainage channel.

### Collection
- Drainage channel → collection tank (500–1,000 L plastic tank)
- Collect daily or after watering
- Use within 24–48 hours for best quality (microbes die if stored too long)

### How to Use

| Use | Dilution | How to Apply |
|---|---|---|
| Spray on leaves (foliar) | 1:10 (1 L tea + 10 L water) | Hand sprayer, apply in evening |
| Pour at plant roots | 1:5 (1 L tea + 5 L water) | Pour around base of plant |
| Drip irrigation | 1:20 | Add to drip tank |

> Never apply undiluted — too strong, can burn plants.

### Selling Worm Tea

| Pack | Price |
|---|---|
| 1 L bottle (ready to use, 1:10 diluted) | ₹80–120/bottle |
| 5 L can (concentrate) | ₹300–500/can |
| 20 L drum (farm use) | ₹1,000–1,500/drum |

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Worms crawling out | Bed too hot, too wet, toxic feed, or ammonia | Check temp + moisture; remove last feed layer; ventilate shed |
| Bad / rotten smell | Overfeeding, meat/dairy added, or too wet | Remove rotten feed; add dry coco peat; turn bed gently |
| Worms sluggish or inactive | Too cold, too dry, or poor feed | Water lightly; add fresh pre-composted dung; check temp |
| Flat, dead worms | Toxic material or extreme heat | Remove all feed immediately; check for chemicals; cool the bed |
| Worms in corners only | Problem in bed centre (heat or chemical) | Check centre temperature; remove problem material; ventilate |
| Ants in bed | Bed too dry, or sugary food scraps | Water bed; remove sweet scraps; put turmeric/ash at bed edges |
| Rats / moles | Wire mesh gap; food near surface | Fix mesh; bury food scraps deep; set snap traps at perimeter |
| Slow output | Underfeeding, low temperature, low worm count | Increase feeding; check worm density; add worms if needed |
| Ammonia smell in bed | Too much N-rich feed or fresh dung used | Add dry coco peat; reduce N feed; ensure pre-composting done |
| Vermicompost too wet | Harvested early or bed over-watered | Spread in shade for 1 day; target 30–40% before bagging |
| White mould on feed | Fungal growth — actinomycetes | Normal, healthy — worms eat this; no action needed |
| Low germination in quality test | Immature product | Cure in open air for 1 week; retest |

## Seasonal Guide

| Month | Key Action |
|---|---|
| **January–February** | Good season. Normal operations. |
| **March** | Start summer prep — check shade net, increase watering frequency |
| **April–May** | High risk. Water beds daily. Mist shade net. Watch for overheating. |
| **June–July** | Monsoon. Cover beds with tarpaulin. Reduce watering. Check drainage. |
| **August–September** | Check for waterlogging after heavy rains. Turn beds if too wet. |
| **October–November** | Best season — ideal temperature and moisture. Expand beds. |
| **November** | Stockpile cow dung and coco peat (cheapest after harvest season). |
| **December** | Slightly slower. Cover beds at night. Add extra gunny sack layer. |

## Quality Check Before Dispatch

Run these checks before bagging any batch.

| Test | How | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| Colour | Look at it | Very dark brown to black |
| Texture | Rub between fingers | Fine, like coffee grounds — no chunky pieces |
| Smell | Smell a handful | Earthy, almost no odour |
| Moisture | Squeeze test | 30–40% for storage (moist but not wet) |
| Germination test | Plant 10 radish seeds in vermicompost + 10 in soil. Check Day 7. | Vermicompost pot ≥ 80% germination of soil pot |

If germination test fails → spread batch in shade for 1 week, retest before bagging.
