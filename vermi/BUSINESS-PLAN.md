# Vermicompost Business Plan
**Farm near Hyderabad, Telangana | Run alongside compost operation**

---

## Quick Summary

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Product | Vermicompost (bagged) + Worm tea (liquid) |
| Start when | Month 6–8 of compost operation (trial), Month 12 (commercial) |
| Starting scale | 10 beds → ~500 kg/month |
| Full scale (Year 2) | 30 beds → ~1,500 kg/month |
| Selling price | ₹20–35/kg (vs ₹5–15/kg for regular compost) |
| Break-even | Month 4–5 of commercial operation |
| Year 2 revenue target | ₹3.6L–5.4L/year from vermicompost alone |

**Why this works alongside compost:**
- Same raw materials (cow dung, crop residues)
- Same customers (nurseries, organic farmers, urban buyers)
- Compost screening rejects feed directly into worm beds — zero waste
- Worm tea = free extra product from beds you already run

## 2. Who Will Buy

### Same customers as your compost business — just higher price segment

**Nurseries** ⭐ Best first customer
- Already buy compost from you — easy upsell
- Customers ask for vermicompost by name
- Want 5–10 kg bags, good appearance, no smell
- Decision is made on the spot by owner
- Target: every nursery account you already have

**Urban Gardeners**
- Hyderabad's terrace/balcony gardening community is large and growing
- Will pay ₹25–35/kg without hesitation for good quality
- Reach: Instagram, Facebook "Hyderabad Gardening" group (85K+ members), apartment RWAs
- Prefer small 5 kg bags — easy to carry

**Organic Vegetable Farmers**
- Already trust you from compost relationship
- Use vermicompost for transplanting seedlings and high-value crops (tomato, capsicum, herbs)
- Buy 25 kg bags or bulk per season

**Input Dealers**
- Stock alongside your bagged compost
- They mark up 25–30% — vermicompost's higher price gives them better margin in absolute rupees

**Institutions (Year 2+)**
- Corporate campus green spaces, hotels with gardens, GHMC nurseries
- Prefer bulk, consistent quality, invoice/GST

## 3. Pricing Strategy

### Why You Can Charge More

- Vermicompost is universally known to be premium — buyers already expect higher price
- Lab test showing NPK numbers justifies price — get one per quarter
- "Made on our own farm" + "worm-processed" = strong story for urban buyers
- You already have trust from the compost relationship — upsell is easy

### Pricing Rules

- Never go below ₹18/kg for any bagged vermicompost — it signals low quality
- Price your 5 kg bag at ₹150–175 (₹30–35/kg) — urban buyers do not price-compare at this level
- Give dealers ₹18–20/kg (they sell at ₹25–28/kg = good margin)
- Bundle deal: customer buys 50 kg compost + 10 kg vermicompost → 5% discount on combined order

## 4. Costs

### Monthly Operating Costs (10 beds, ~500 kg/month output)

| Item | Monthly Cost |
|---|---|
| Feed — cow dung (100–150 kg/week × 4 weeks) | ₹2,000–3,000 |
| Feed — coco peat, dry leaves, crop waste | ₹1,000–1,500 |
| Water + electricity (misting, lighting) | ₹500–800 |
| Labour (shared with compost operator, ~1 hr/day) | ₹3,000–4,000 |
| Bags (500 bags, assorted sizes) | ₹2,500–4,000 |
| Worm tea bottles/cans | ₹1,000–1,500 |
| Maintenance + misc | ₹500–1,000 |
| **Total monthly OpEx** | **₹10,500–15,800** |
| **Round figure** | **~₹13,000/month** |

**Cost per kg of vermicompost:** ₹13,000 ÷ 500 kg = **~₹26/kg**

> At 500 kg/month this looks tight. The key is to scale to 30 beds where fixed costs spread further — cost drops to ₹10–14/kg and margin improves significantly.

### Monthly Operating Costs (30 beds, ~1,500 kg/month output)

| Item | Monthly Cost |
|---|---|
| Feed (all types) | ₹7,000–10,000 |
| Labour (1 dedicated operator, part-time) | ₹8,000–10,000 |
| Water + electricity | ₹1,500–2,000 |
| Bags + bottles | ₹7,000–9,000 |
| Maintenance + misc | ₹1,500–2,000 |
| **Total monthly OpEx** | **₹25,000–33,000** |
| **Round figure** | **~₹29,000/month** |

**Cost per kg at 30 beds:** ₹29,000 ÷ 1,500 kg = **~₹19/kg**

## 12. Risks & Fixes

| Risk | How Likely | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Worms die in summer heat | High if no shade | Shade net mandatory; water beds daily in Apr–May |
| Slow output in Year 1 | Medium | Normal — worm population takes 3–4 months to build; be patient |
| Customers don't see difference from compost | Low | Always demo with germination test; show lab NPK comparison |
| Raw material cost rises | Low | You share the same supply chain as compost — bulk negotiation covers both |
| Worm population crash | Low | Keep 10–20% of beds as backup "worm reserve" — never harvest all worms from a bed |
| Vermicompost too wet when bagged | Medium | Spread in shade for 1 day before bagging; target 30–40% moisture |
