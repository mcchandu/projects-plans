# Commercial Vermicompost Unit – 20 Bed Capacity

## Overview

This repository contains the conceptual design and feasibility plan for a **commercial vermicompost manufacturing unit** near Hyderabad, Telangana, India.

The unit uses agricultural residues, cow dung, farm organic waste and pre-composted material. The design includes **20 vermicompost beds**, pre-composting, processing, harvesting, screening, storage and bagging.

> **Important:** This is a vermicompost unit. The vermicompost beds use earthworms. Fresh organic material must first be pre-composted and cooled.

# 2. Why Pre-Composting Is Required

Fresh organic material may generate excessive heat and ammonia, which can harm earthworms.

```text
FRESH COW DUNG + CROP WASTE
            ↓
     PRE-COMPOSTING
        15–20 DAYS
            ↓
      MATERIAL COOLS
            ↓
    VERMICOMPOST BED
```

Before loading a vermicompost bed, material should be cooled, moist, well aerated and free from strong ammonia smell.

# 7. Bed Operation

## Step 1 — Prepare Feedstock

Possible materials:

- Cow dung
- Chopped crop residues
- Dry leaves
- Green farm waste

## Step 2 — Pre-Compost

Maintain the material for approximately **15–20 days**.

Monitor:

- Temperature
- Moisture
- Smell

## Step 3 — Cool Material

Before placing material into beds:

- Ensure excessive heat has reduced
- Ensure strong ammonia smell is absent
- Maintain suitable moisture

## Step 4 — Load Beds

Avoid:

- Over-compaction
- Excess water
- Fresh hot manure

## Step 5 — Introduce Earthworms

Maintain:

- Shade
- Moisture
- Aeration
- Suitable temperature

## Step 6 — Monitor

Regularly check:

- Moisture
- Temperature
- Earthworm activity
- Ants and pests
- Bad smell

## Step 7 — Harvest

Harvest mature vermicompost, separate worms where practical, screen the product and store it in a dry covered area.

---

# 8. Moisture Management

## Too Dry

Problems:

- Reduced earthworm activity
- Slow processing

Action:

- Light watering
- Frequent monitoring

## Too Wet

Problems:

- Low oxygen
- Bad smell
- Earthworm stress

Action:

- Improve drainage
- Reduce watering
- Add suitable dry material if required

# 10. Quality Checks

Good vermicompost should generally be:

- Dark brown to black
- Loose and crumbly
- Earthy smelling
- Free from plastic and contamination

Track:

- Moisture
- Particle size
- Smell
- Maturity
- Batch details

For commercial sale, laboratory testing and applicable Indian fertilizer/product standards should be verified before making nutrient or quality claims.
